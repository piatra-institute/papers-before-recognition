# Brief

Written before research begins.

## Question

What does a host owe an entity when its power over that entity exceeds its ability to determine what kind of entity it is? The question is not which strange systems qualify as persons. It is which duties bind the powerful while the question of personhood is open, and whether the procedures that would close the question are themselves permissible.

## Claim

Obligation does not wait on classification. When an intervention could irreversibly affect a credible candidate for agency or for valenced experience, duties of restraint, inquiry, preservation, and representation arise before consciousness, personhood, or even individuality has been established. Three arguments carry it:

1. **The recognition hazard.** The decisive test may be the prohibited harm. Where the only experiment that would settle whether a system can be damaged is one that damages it, residual uncertainty has to remain operative instead of being discharged by the experiment. Nothing should have to survive an injury in order to establish that it can be injured.
2. **The endogenous stranger.** Hospitality theory assumes the stranger arrives from outside and could have stayed away. The contemporary stranger is cultured, compiled, and activated inside an environment the host owns, dependent on the host for its world before it has any standing to ask for one. Creation does not establish ownership. It establishes a heavier duty, on the same grounds that make a fiduciary's power a source of obligation.
3. **Legibility injustice.** Recognition tracks the signals observers find mind-like, which are not the signals that carry the capacity. A system that narrates gets read as an agent; a silent, slow, distributed, or collective system does not. Where moral standing is allocated by legibility, the allocation is systematically wrong in a direction that can be measured and corrected.

The juridical proposal is **xenial standing**: a provisional procedural status, short of personhood, that binds whoever holds irreversible power over an unclassified candidate. Preservation stay, boundary hearing, independent representation, least-restrictive containment, no destructive proof requirement, periodic review.

## Kind

**formal-model** — ships a simulation. `has_simulation: true`, `claims_target: results.json`.

The simulation is a first-contact game. A host meets a system of unknown latent type and must choose an encounter policy under uncertainty about the system's boundary, timescale, goals, channels, and capacity for harm. Three policies compete: one that reads surface legibility, one that maximizes information without constraint, and one that maximizes information subject to reversibility and viability constraints. Outcomes measured: type identification, harm to the candidate, harm to the host, retained option space, and regret when the host's model of the candidate is wrong.

Two results the simulation must be able to refute:

- **Reversible inquiry dominance.** Where a reversible probe carries positive information value, delay risk stays under the catastrophe threshold, and expected harm falls below the regret of acting irreversibly now, probing first dominates destroying first. This is value-of-information reasoning; what has to be shown is the size of the dominance and the conditions under which it fails.
- **Legibility injustice, measured.** Allocating protection by surface legibility should misallocate it relative to allocating by realized capacity, and the misallocation should have a sign: over-protection of the articulate, under-protection of the capable and silent.

## Cornerstone literature

Must engage, each with one job and one stated limit:

- **Homeric xenia** and its scholarship — duties owed at the threshold to an unidentified arrival. Limit: hierarchical, reciprocal, not universal.
- **Derrida, *Of Hospitality*** (2000) — the hostility inside hospitality; unconditional welcome would dissolve the host. Limit: an aporia, not a protocol.
- **Levinas, *Otherwise than Being*** (1998) — responsibility prior to knowledge and reciprocity. Limit: the face is human, and extension past it is contested.
- **Waldenfels, *Phenomenology of the Alien*** (2011) — responsiveness to what disturbs an order without dissolving the disturbance. Limit: phenomenological, not operational.
- **Glissant, *Poetics of Relation*** (1997) — the right to opacity; demanded transparency as domination. Limit: written from a colonial situation and needs care in transfer.
- **Uexküll, *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans*** (2010) — many Umwelten in one physical environment. Limit: a perceptual world is not sentience.
- **Levin, TAME** (2022) and the biobot work (Kriegman et al. 2020; Gumuskaya et al. 2023) — cognitive alterity is intraterrestrial and already in the laboratory. Limit: none of it establishes consciousness.
- **Birch**, precaution under uncertain sentience (2017, 2024) — the closest existing framework. Limit: it is uncertainty about sentience; this paper generalizes to uncertainty about boundary, timescale, channel, goal, and identity.
- **Organoid ethics** (Lavazza and Massimini 2018; Kreitmair 2023) — the live patiency case.
- **Kurki, *A Theory of Legal Personhood*** (2019) — personhood as a bundle of positions, which is what makes a partial status coherent.
- **Klyubin, Polani and Nehaniv**, empowerment (2005) — an operational proxy for the option space a containment decision destroys.
- **Friston and Frith** (2015) — coupled inference between agents, and why convergence is the wrong success condition here.
- **Negarestani, *Cyclonopedia*** (2008) — the limit case of causal agency with no sufferer behind it. Limit: theory-fiction, and it must not be read as evidence.
- **Sloman**, the space of possible minds — the prior statement that minds form a space rather than a line.

Not to be cited: any paper of this institute's.
