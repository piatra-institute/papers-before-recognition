## v1 (2026-08-02) — written from the seed

Seeded from a research conversation proposing a foundational ethics for encounters with radically unfamiliar minds. The seed was strong on structure and overbuilt on apparatus; the reductions are recorded in `research.md` and are the main editorial decision here.

**Cut from the seed.** A six-term encounter functional that priced harm, domination, irreversibility and information gain on one scale and optimised, with no argument for the exchange rates; an interventional translation-deficit measure requiring a library of familiar agent models, a space of admissible ontology translations, and a description-length penalty, each of which is a research programme rather than a definition; a numeric formula for activating legal standing. The structural claim behind the last is kept in words, since the product of power, irreversibility and expected harm is doing real work and a number would be false precision. Four of the seed's seven principle names were dropped: a paper arriving with seven coinages asks the reader to learn a vocabulary before it has earned one. Three are kept because they name things the paper measures or proposes: the recognition hazard, legibility injustice, xenial standing.

**The corpus constraint.** The seed built its empirical section directly on a measurement framework from a sibling paper, notation included. Papers here stand alone, so the machinery was re-derived in this paper's terms and claimed only for its ethical use. The evidence-against-realization gap enters as a normative diagnosis, not as a contribution to attribution theory, and the simulation measures the injustice directly through competing policies, which needs no attribution machinery.

**The simulation.** A first-contact game, five latent types, three policies, 4000 encounters each. The one load-bearing modelling choice is that valence is reachable only through the destructive probe, which puts the recognition hazard into the instrument instead of asserting it about the world; the paper states this where it introduces the game.

Two design faults were found and fixed in the building. The first version let valence be inferred without the destructive probe, which erased the paper's central difficulty, and had a surface policy whose destroy branch never fired, so the legibility result could not appear. The observation model was rebuilt around per-act channels to fix both. The second fault was more interesting. The constrained policy had no stopping rule, and because it refuses the measurement that resolves valence it never resolves, so it probed to the round limit and paid for every round; it lost to the surface policy in 11 of 15 regimes. A stopping rule that halts inquiry when the last probe bought less than 0.05 nats took it to 14 of 15. That is a finding rather than a tuning fix, and it is now reported beside the dominance claim with its own measurement: patience wins in 3 of 3 delay regimes with the rule and 1 of 3 without.

**Sources.** Fifteen cited, every one verified against Crossref or OpenLibrary before use. Dropped: the New York Declaration (not peer-reviewed, and Birch carries the point with a locator), Sloman (cited in the seed to an archive PDF, and load-bearing for nothing), Haraway and Deleuze and Guattari (listed in the seed and never needed), Klyubin and Polani (verified, unused once retained option space was defined directly). The seed's two-page cultural prologue reading a horror series was cut entirely; the structure it illustrated is stated in one paragraph without it.
